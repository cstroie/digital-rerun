TIDEPOOL
========

A very slow garden you can't control.

Ten rocks and a tide table. Things grow when the tide is in, and the only
thing you decide is what not to disturb. It is an idle game built around
the clock: the pool keeps real time, so come back after a week away and
the pool has changed. Nothing here can be won, lost or hurried.

  TIDEPOOL          asks for your terminal (the first time)
  TIDEPOOL /100     VT100      /52  VT52      /ADM  ADM-3A

The clock
  The tide takes 12 hours 25 minutes, high water to high water, counted
  from the machine's date and time. While you are away the tides go on:
  fourteen of them in a week. The next time you start, the pool is
  brought up to date tide by tide (at most 2000 of them, nearly three
  years) and TIDEPOOL tells you what happened. On a machine without a
  clock the pool counts one tide for each visit.

The screen
  rows 1-3    day of the pool, the hour, the tide and its gauge (15 is high
              water); a wave rule
  rows 4-15   the pool. The sun or the moon crosses the sky. The water
              stands as high as the tide: at high water it covers the
              rocks, at low water the creatures come out into the air
  rows 16-18  the sand (a cairn _/^^\_ where you left a rock alone), the
              names of the ten rocks, your cairn stones
  rows 19-22  the rock you are looking at: what lives on it, how long it
              has been quiet
  row 23      the latest happening        row 24  the keys

The seasons
  The real calendar sets the pace. Winter (December to February): growth
  is halved, little settles, storms are common. Spring: things settle
  eagerly. Summer: growth is a quarter faster, storms are rare, crabs
  wander in. Autumn: storms return. The season is named on row 1.

What lives there
  algae       slick film on bare rock; the start of everything
  barnacles   slow, and tough
  kelp        fast; a full grown stand may let go; urchins find it
  mussels     slow; a sea star finds them on a rock that stays quiet
  anemone     opens on a barnacle rock left quiet for 12 days
  sea star    arrives, eats, moves on
  hermit crab and urchin   visitors; they cross a rock and go
  A storm tide now and then tears barnacles, kelp and mussels off.
  Each picture has four sizes: tiny, young, grown, full grown.

What you decide
  You have five cairn stones. SPACE puts one on the rock you are looking
  at (or lifts it). A rock with a cairn is left alone.
  When you leave (Q), you wade out across the rocks that have no cairn:
  what lives there is stepped on, set back a quarter and a little more,
  and its quiet starts again from nothing; visitors are scared off.
  The rare things (anemones, sea stars) need quiet: days of it. Visit
  often and walk the same rocks and only the common things win. Choose
  the five you will leave alone, and go away for a while.

Keys
  left/right, < >, 1-9 and 0   choose a rock (arrow keys, ^S and ^D too)
  SPACE or Enter               put a cairn on it, or lift it
  N                            name the rock (seven letters)
  T                            the tide table: a day of the tide drawn
                               out, the next high and low waters
  J                            the journal: the last sixteen happenings
                               (a rock is named as it was named then)
  B                            the bell on or off: it rings when a tide
                               turns while you watch, and at low water
  R                            start a new pool (asks first: Y)
  ?                            this page, short
  Q                            leave (saves, and walks the open rocks)

If you close the page without Q, the next start walks the open rocks
anyway (and says so): leaving is never free. When you come back after a
while, TIDEPOOL tells you how long, how many tides, which rocks changed
and what happened.

The pool is kept in TIDEPOOL.SAV on the disk you ran it from, and saved on
every cairn, every name and every tide. Delete it to start a new pool.
